# Phase 9 — Case Studies II: Booking, Commerce & Finance

**Why here:** these are the domains of the target companies (PhonePe, Razorpay, Flipkart, Swiggy, Uber,
Amazon, Walmart) and the ones where **concurrency and money correctness** decide the score: seats,
rooms and meeting slots (double booking), wallets and ledgers (double spend, lost updates, idempotent
retries), order books (price-time priority), carts and coupons (rules that change weekly), inventory
(oversell), and matching (a driver assigned twice). Needs Phases 0–8, especially 7.3.5.
**Case-study rows:** as in Phase 8 (`Hook` = the prompt; `Derive` = the mentor's private map, never
shown before the attempt; `Adapt` = curveballs).
**Scaffolding:** interview conditions: cold, company-styled, hints cost points everywhere.
**Money rule for the whole phase:** integer minor units in a `Money` value type; no `double` anywhere
near money (a `double` costs *code quality* and *entity modelling* points on sight).

| 9.1.1 | MC | **BookMyShow (movie ticket booking)** — Hook: "Design a movie ticket booking system like BookMyShow: cities, theatres, shows, seat selection, booking and payment. Implement booking end to end." · Derive: `City` → `Theatre` → `Screen` (seat layout) → `Show` (movie, screen, time) → `ShowSeat` (per-show seat state: Available/Held/Booked, price category); `Booking` state machine (Pending → Confirmed / Expired / Cancelled); **`SeatHoldService`**: hold selected seats atomically for N minutes, confirm on payment, release on expiry (injected clock); `PaymentGateway` interface; `PricingStrategy` (category, time of day, dynamic); search by city/movie/date (Specification or simple queries); the hard part: **atomic multi-seat hold** under concurrency (per-show lock or ordered per-seat locks; 7.3.5) and the payment-after-expiry race · Covers: browse shows, view seat map, hold seats (all-or-nothing), confirm with payment, hold expiry, cancel with refund policy (Strategy), no double booking under 32 concurrent users (stress test), tests for hold/confirm/expire/cancel paths · Adapt: group booking must be adjacent seats; dynamic pricing when occupancy > 70%; a payment callback arrives after the hold expired and the seats were resold; admin cancels a show (refund all, notify) · Anchors: among the most-asked LLD questions (Flipkart, Amazon, Microsoft, Uber, Swiggy); the seat-locking discussion is the core of the round · When-NOT: a global lock around booking; modelling movies' cast/reviews; Singleton `BookingService` · File: `designs/phase-09/bookmyshow/` |
| 9.1.2 | MC | **Hotel Booking** — Hook: "Design a hotel reservation system: room types, availability for date ranges, booking, cancellation. Implement search and booking." · Derive: `Hotel` → `RoomType` (capacity, base rate) → `Room`; `Reservation` over a `DateRange` value object (half-open `[checkIn, checkOut)`), availability = rooms of the type with no overlapping reservation (interval overlap test; per-room sorted reservations or a per-date inventory count per type); `RatePlan`/`PricingStrategy` (weekday/weekend/seasonal, per-night sum); `CancellationPolicy` Strategy (free until 48 h, then first night); the hard part: availability queries by *type* for a range and booking atomically when two guests want the last room of a type · Covers: search availability (type, dates, guests), book (assign a room at check-in or at booking? ask), modify dates, cancel with policy, overlapping-range correctness (boundary: checkout day = next check-in), concurrency test for the last room · Adapt: overbooking allowance of 5% per type; room upgrades when a type is full; multi-room booking all-or-nothing; a hotel chain with loyalty points · Anchors: common LLD question (Amazon, Walmart, Expedia-style), interval reasoning · When-NOT: per-day objects for 10 years of dates; a booking engine that knows about payments' internals · File: `designs/phase-09/hotel-booking/` |
| 9.1.3 | MC | **Meeting-Room Scheduler / Calendar** — Hook: "Build a meeting scheduler for an office: rooms with capacities, people with calendars. Schedule a meeting, invite people, find free slots." · Derive: `Room` (capacity, amenities), `Person`/`Calendar` (sorted busy intervals), `Meeting` (organiser, attendees, `TimeSlot`, room, status), `Invitation` with RSVP; conflict detection = interval overlap per room and per attendee (a sorted `std::set`/`TreeMap` keyed by start for O(log n) checks); **free-slot finder** = merge attendees' busy intervals and scan gaps within working hours; `RoomSelectionStrategy` (smallest fitting, preferred floor); the hard part: booking a room atomically while checking attendees and the free-slot algorithm's correctness · Covers: create/cancel meetings, room booking without conflicts, attendee conflict warnings, find the earliest common free slot of length d for k people within a window, notifications via an interface, tests for touching-boundary intervals and the slot finder · Adapt: recurring meetings (weekly, with one cancelled instance); time zones for a remote attendee; a room goes under maintenance and its meetings must move; two organisers book the last room at the same instant · Anchors: Uber/Atlassian/Microsoft-style LLD and coding (Merge Intervals, Meeting Rooms II), Google Calendar-lite · When-NOT: modelling the whole corporate directory; an iCal parser · File: `designs/phase-09/meeting-scheduler/` |

## Cycle 9.2 — Money

| ID | Type | Session |
|---|---|---|
| 9.2.1 | MC | **Splitwise** — Hook: "Design Splitwise: users add expenses split among people in different ways; show who owes whom; settle up. Implement it." · Derive: `User`, `Group`, `Expense` (payer(s), amount as `Money`, participants, split), **`SplitStrategy`** (Equal, Exact, Percent, Shares) validated at creation (percents sum to 100, exact sums to total, fair distribution of leftover paise); a **balance sheet** (net balance per user, or pairwise map) updated per expense; **simplify debts**: net balances → greedy match of max creditor with max debtor (two heaps) gives ≤ n−1 transactions; the true minimum is NP-hard (subset-sum structure): say so; the hard part: rounding correctness and choosing pairwise vs net-balance representation · Covers: add users/groups, add expense with each split type, show balances (per user, per pair), settle-up payments, simplify debts, expense history per group, tests for each split (incl. ₹100 among 3), simplification correctness (sum of balances = 0 invariant) · Adapt: multi-currency expenses with conversion at a rate on the expense date; editing/deleting an expense (recompute or reverse entries?); recurring expenses; concurrent expense additions to one group (lock per group) · Anchors: very common in Indian machine-coding rounds (Flipkart, PhonePe, Razorpay, Swiggy) · When-NOT: floating-point percentages; a graph library for debt simplification; a `SplitFactory` with 4 levels · File: `designs/phase-09/splitwise/` |
| 9.2.2 | MC | **Payment Wallet (PhonePe/Paytm-style)** — Hook: "Design a digital wallet: users add money from a bank or card, pay merchants, send money to other users, and see their transaction history. Implement the core." · Derive: `Wallet` (owner, balance as `Money`, status, limits by KYC tier), **ledger** of immutable `Transaction`s / double-entry `LedgerEntry` pairs (every movement debits one account and credits another; balance = sum of entries, or a cached balance reconciled with the ledger), `TransferService` with **idempotency keys** (a retried request returns the original result), **lock ordering by wallet ID** for P2P transfers (6.3.1), `FundingSource` adapters for bank/card/UPI providers (Adapter 3.1.1) with a payment **state machine** (Initiated → Pending → Success/Failed → Refunded); the hard part: correctness under concurrency and retries: no double spend, no lost credit, refunds that reverse exactly · Covers: create wallet, add money (provider success/failure/timeout), P2P transfer (insufficient balance, limits), merchant payment, refund (full/partial), history with filters, idempotent retries, a stress test (32 threads of random transfers: total money conserved, no negative balance), tests for each failure path · Adapt: cashback offers credited after payment (rules: 4.3.5-lite); KYC tier limits per day/month; a provider callback arrives twice or late; freeze a wallet under investigation mid-transfer · Anchors: PhonePe/Paytm/Razorpay machine-coding and LLD rounds; Amazon Pay · When-NOT: `double` balances; a mutable balance with no ledger; locking the whole wallet service; distributed transactions in a machine-coding round (say where they'd go) · File: `designs/phase-09/payment-wallet/` |
| 9.2.3 | LD | **LLD discussion: Payment Gateway Integration & Routing (Razorpay-style)** — Hook: "A merchant platform integrates 4 payment providers. Design the component that creates payments, routes each to a provider, handles callbacks and retries, and reports status. Let's discuss the design; code the critical parts." · Derive: `Payment` aggregate with a strict **state machine** (Created → Authorised → Captured → Refunded / Failed), `ProviderAdapter` per provider (Adapter), **`RoutingStrategy`** (by method, success rate, cost, provider health: circuit-breaker-lite), idempotency on create and on callbacks, retry with backoff for provider timeouts vs never retrying a non-idempotent charge, reconciliation job for unknown outcomes; the hard part: timeouts with unknown outcome (did the charge happen?) and duplicate/out-of-order callbacks · Covers: requirements, class diagram, the state machine with every transition's guard, interfaces with signatures, sequence diagram for create → redirect → callback → capture, routing strategy design, idempotency design, failure handling table (timeout, duplicate callback, provider down, partial refund), extensibility (a 5th provider, a new method like UPI Autopay) · Adapt: a provider's success rate drops to 40% for UPI in the evening (route away); a refund fails at the provider after the merchant was told it succeeded · Anchors: Razorpay/PhonePe/Juspay-style LLD rounds (reported), Stripe API design · When-NOT: designing the card network; building a generic workflow engine for 5 states · File: `designs/phase-09/payment-gateway/` |
| 9.2.4 | MC | **Stock Broker Order Matching Engine** — Hook: "Build an order matching engine for a stock exchange: traders place buy and sell orders; matching orders execute as trades. Support limit and market orders. Implement it." · Derive: `Order` (id, trader, symbol, side, type, price, quantity, remaining, timestamp), an **`OrderBook` per symbol**: bids as `std::map<Price, std::deque<Order>, std::greater<>>`, asks as `std::map<Price, std::deque<Order>>` (price levels with FIFO queues: **price-time priority**), an order-ID → location index for O(1)/O(log n) cancel, `MatchingEngine` producing `Trade`s, `TradeListener`s (Observer); prices as integer ticks; the hard part: matching loop correctness (partial fills across levels, market orders sweeping the book, remaining quantity) and cancel/modify semantics (modify price = cancel + new, loses priority) · Covers: place limit/market orders, match with partial fills, cancel, modify quantity (down keeps priority), order-book snapshot (top 5 levels), trade log, per-symbol independence, tests for price-time priority, partial fills, market order with insufficient liquidity (reject remainder or cancel?), cancel of a partially filled order · Adapt: stop-loss orders (triggered by last trade price: a separate trigger book); IOC and FOK order types; per-symbol threading (one thread/actor per book, 7.3.4) with a concurrent order-entry front; circuit breaker halting a symbol on a 10% move · Anchors: Zerodha/fintech LLD rounds, Uber/Goldman-style design questions, "design a stock exchange" (the LLD half) · When-NOT: floating-point prices; one global lock for all symbols; modelling settlement and clearing · File: `designs/phase-09/order-matching/` |
| 9.2.5 | R | Cycle review: explain-back of double-entry + idempotency + lock ordering; rebuild-back the order-book structure and matching loop in 10 min; the money-correctness checklist written by the learner into `reference/case-study-index.md` (a cross-case section) |

## Cycle 9.3 — Commerce & mobility

| ID | Type | Session |
|---|---|---|
| 9.3.1 | MC | **Shopping Cart, Checkout & Coupon/Discount Engine (Amazon/Flipkart-style)** — Hook: "Design the cart and checkout for an e-commerce site, including coupons and discounts. Marketing will keep inventing new offer types. Implement cart, pricing and coupon application." · Derive: `Cart` (lines of `ProductId` × qty; prices *looked up*, not stored), `Product`/`Catalogue`, a **pricing pipeline** (base prices → item-level offers → cart-level coupons → shipping → tax) as ordered stages (4.3.4), `Coupon`/`Offer` = **condition (Specification, 4.3.2) + benefit (Strategy)** + metadata (validity, usage limits, stackable/exclusive group, priority), an `OfferEngine` choosing applicable offers with a conflict-resolution strategy and producing an **explained** price breakdown; checkout creates an immutable `OrderSnapshot` (1.2.5) and reserves inventory; the hard part: adding a new offer type ("buy 2 get 1 on shoes") without editing the engine, and deterministic stacking rules · Covers: add/remove/update cart lines, price the cart with a breakdown, apply/remove coupon codes (validation: expiry, min cart value, first order, usage count), automatic offers, stacking and exclusivity, checkout to an order snapshot, tests per offer type and for stacking order · Adapt: BOGO on a category (new condition + benefit only); bank-card offers applied at payment time (a stage after payment selection); coupon usage limit per user under concurrent checkouts (atomic increment with check); a price change between cart view and checkout · Anchors: Amazon/Flipkart/Walmart/Myntra LLD rounds (coupon engines are a favourite) · When-NOT: a full rules engine DSL in 90 min (a condition+benefit interface is enough); storing computed prices in the cart · File: `designs/phase-09/cart-coupons/` |
| 9.3.2 | MC | **Inventory Management** — Hook: "Design an inventory management system for an e-commerce company with multiple warehouses: stock levels, reservations for orders, replenishment, low-stock alerts. Implement it." · Derive: `Sku`, `Warehouse`, `StockLevel` (on-hand, reserved, available = on-hand − reserved) per (warehouse, SKU), **reservation lifecycle** (Reserve on checkout → Commit on shipment / Release on cancel or TTL expiry), `AllocationStrategy` for choosing warehouses (nearest to customer, most stock, split shipments allowed?), `StockMovement` ledger (inbound, outbound, adjustment, transfer: auditability like 9.2.2), low-stock **observers**, replenishment rules (reorder point, reorder quantity); the hard part: **no oversell** under concurrent reservations (per-SKU-warehouse lock or CAS on available) and multi-warehouse allocation atomically · Covers: add stock, reserve for an order (multi-line, all-or-nothing), commit, release, TTL expiry of reservations, transfer between warehouses, low-stock alerts, stock report, stress test (concurrent reservations never drive available below 0), tests per lifecycle path · Adapt: split an order across two warehouses when neither has all items; perishable goods with batch expiry (FEFO: first-expired-first-out); a cycle count finds a discrepancy (adjustment with audit); flash sale on one SKU (hot key: 7.2.3) · Anchors: Flipkart/Amazon/Walmart/Swiggy Instamart LLD rounds · When-NOT: one lock for the whole inventory; mutable stock numbers with no movement log · File: `designs/phase-09/inventory/` |
| 9.3.3 | MC | **Food Delivery (Swiggy/Zomato-style)** — Hook: "Design a food delivery platform: customers browse restaurants and menus, place orders, restaurants accept and prepare, delivery partners pick up and deliver. Implement the order flow and partner assignment." · Derive: `Restaurant` (menu of `MenuItem`s with availability, open hours), `Order` with an explicit **state machine** (Placed → Accepted → Preparing → ReadyForPickup → PickedUp → Delivered; Cancelled with state-dependent refund rules), `DeliveryPartner` (location, status, current order), **`PartnerAssignmentStrategy`** (nearest available, least busy, rating-aware; with a timeout and reassignment if not accepted), `PricingStrategy`/fees (distance, surge), order events → notifications (event bus 4.3.3); the hard part: assignment concurrency (a partner offered two orders at once; accept races) and state-dependent cancellation · Covers: search restaurants (by cuisine, rating, open now), place order (item availability, restaurant open), restaurant accept/reject, assign partner with accept/decline/timeout, status tracking, cancellation and refunds by state, ratings, tests for the state machine and assignment races · Adapt: order batching (a partner carries two orders from the same restaurant); surge pricing when partners are scarce; scheduled orders for later; a restaurant runs out of an item after acceptance (partial fulfilment) · Anchors: Swiggy/Zomato/Uber Eats LLD rounds; Uber SDE-2 (reported) · When-NOT: real geospatial indexing (use a simple distance; mention geohash as the system-design follow-up); a microservice per entity · File: `designs/phase-09/food-delivery/` |
| 9.3.4 | MC | **Ride Sharing (Uber/Ola-style)** — Hook: "Design a ride-hailing system: riders request rides, the system matches a nearby driver, the trip happens, fare is charged. Implement matching and the trip lifecycle." · Derive: `Rider`, `Driver` (vehicle, location, status: Offline/Available/OnTrip), `RideRequest`, `Trip` **state machine** (Requested → DriverAssigned → DriverArrived → InProgress → Completed / Cancelled with fees), **`MatchingStrategy`** (nearest available within radius, then by rating/ETA; offer to one driver at a time with timeout, or broadcast to k), **`FareStrategy`** (base + per-km + per-min, surge multiplier by demand/supply per zone, vehicle-type rates), `Payment` via an interface; the hard part: **a driver must never be assigned two trips**: atomic check-and-assign on driver status (per-driver lock or CAS on status) while many requests race, and cancellation after assignment · Covers: driver goes online/updates location, rider requests (vehicle type), match with accept/decline/timeout and re-match, trip start/end with fare, cancellation rules (rider/driver, before/after arrival), ratings, trip history, concurrency test (100 concurrent requests, 20 drivers: no driver double-assigned, every request matched or rejected) · Adapt: pool/shared rides (two riders, compatible routes, fare split); scheduled rides; surge zones recomputed every minute; driver's app goes offline mid-trip · Anchors: Uber/Ola SDE-2 LLD (reported), common in Indian rounds · When-NOT: routing/ETA algorithms (stub them); geospatial indexes beyond a grid bucket; payments internals · File: `designs/phase-09/ride-sharing/` |
